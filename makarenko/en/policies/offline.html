“Offline Project: Working Together in Person” Policy approved

Full text of the policy · Pay and Employee Programmes

The main drawback of remote work is that team members do not meet in person.

Remote employees can work together for years without knowing each other by sight, and part ways having kept nothing but chat history. This weakens the trust and mutual understanding on which joint work rests.

This policy introduces annual in-person joint work. Its purpose: to improve mutual understanding in the team, speed up the transfer of experience from senior to junior engineers, and give employees a shared experience they will remember.

General principle

Once a year, in one of the summer months, a group of engineers gathers for two weeks in Innopolis, a city built for IT specialists, and works together on one or more production tasks.

The senior member of the group shares experience with the juniors. The group works in the open office of the Popov Technopark. Accommodation is provided for the duration of the trip.

The original document includes photos of the workspace and examples of accommodation.

Programme No. 1 (network engineers)

Examples of joint production tasks for network engineers:

  1. Simulating typical customer requests to technical support;
  2. Setting up network demo stands;
  3. Charity projects that also introduce people to the enterprise (e.g. connecting a village school or museum to the internet);
  4. Practising teamwork skills.

Programme No. 2 (electronics engineers, embedded programmers)

Examples of joint production tasks for microelectronics specialists and embedded programmers:

  1. Training in electronic instruments (oscilloscope, soldering station, etc.);
  2. Setting up electronic demo stands;
  3. Charity projects that also introduce people to the enterprise (e.g. a public seminar on the company's automation systems);
  4. Practising teamwork skills.

Programme No. 3 (web developers for IoT, IIoT)

Examples of joint production tasks for IoT and IIoT web developers:

  1. Sharing experience in building web interfaces for IoT and IIoT;
  2. Setting up device-monitoring web servers;
  3. Charity projects that also introduce people to the enterprise (e.g. a business-card website for a village museum);
  4. Practising teamwork skills.

Organisation

Participation is voluntary. The group's composition, dates and tasks are agreed with the manager in advance, no later than two months before the trip. The trip is arranged as a business trip: the enterprise pays for travel and accommodation.

Evaluation

After the trip the group presents the results of its tasks to management, and participants fill in a short questionnaire about what working in person gave them. Based on this, next year's programme is adjusted.
