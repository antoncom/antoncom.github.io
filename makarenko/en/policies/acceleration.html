TeamTime Acceleration — Description applied when hiring

Full text of the policy · Pay and Employee Programmes

This policy describes the pay system used in the development department. Its goal is to make salary growth predictable and transparent by linking it to the employee's experience as confirmed by daily reports.

Definition

Literal: from Latin acceleratio — acceleration.

In the context of the TeamTime methodology, acceleration is a form of pay whose essential feature is a consistent increase in salary depending on the experience the employee accumulates in their specialty.

Below, we use the term “TeamTime Acceleration” for acceleration in this sense.

Characteristics

TeamTime Acceleration resembles several well-known forms of pay, but coincides with none of them.

What TeamTime Acceleration is not

  • Piece-rate pay, where the employee is paid for the quantity of output (work, services) produced.
  • Piece-rate-plus-bonus pay, where bonuses are paid in addition to the salary.
  • Progressive piece-rate pay, where piece rates depend on the amount of output over a given period.
  • Indirect piece-rate pay, used for staff in service and auxiliary units, whose earnings depend on the pay of piece-rate workers in the main production.

Essential features of TeamTime Acceleration

  1. Pay consists of two parts: base and piece-rate. The base is the fixed part of the salary that is never reduced. The piece-rate is the variable part.
  2. The piece-rate part is formed on the basis of work actually done, confirmed by reports stating the time actually spent.
  3. The monthly piece-rate amount is calculated at the hourly rate stated in the employee's employment (or apprenticeship) contract.
  4. The employee's hourly rate is determined mainly by relevant education, experience in the specialty and experience gained at this enterprise.
  5. Education and experience are taken into account by a single formula for everyone (see below).

Formula for the piece-rate hourly rate

hRate = Function(eduYears, eduQuality, seniorityYears, timerecordMonths, skillHours)

Meaning of the indicators

IndicatorMeaning
hRatehourly rate for the piece-rate part of pay (roubles);
eduYearsyears of education in the specialty (5 for university, 3 for technical college);
eduQualityaverage grade on the final transcript (from 3 to 5; diploma with honours = 5);
seniorityYearstotal work experience in the specialty at the time of joining this enterprise;
timerecordMonthslength of service at this enterprise, counted not in calendar months but in actual hours at 120 hours = 1 month;
skillHoursexperience in the skills of the specialty (from 0 to N, where N = 0 for a student; N ≥ 720 for Junior; N ≥ 1440 for Middle; N = 2880 for Senior).

Below is a draft of the formula. The rate grid (hourly rates R by level) and the coefficients are approved by management; the coefficient values are given as examples.

  • The employee's level is determined by skillHours: intern (0–719), Junior (720–1439), Middle (1440–2879), Senior (from 2880).
  • Within a level, the base hourly rate grows evenly from the rate of the current level to that of the next:
Rbase = Rlevel + (Rnext − Rlevel) × (skillHours − Hlevel) / (Hnext − Hlevel)

Here H is the skillHours threshold for a level. For the Senior level Rbase = Rsenior; further growth is decided by the manager.

  • Education premium: Kedu = 1 + 0.02 × eduYears × (eduQuality − 3) / 2. For example, a university diploma with honours gives Kedu = 1.10; a technical college with an average grade of 4 gives Kedu = 1.03.
  • Tenure premium at this enterprise: Ktenure = 1 + 0.01 × min(timerecordMonths / 12; 5), i.e. up to +5% over five years.
  • Experience in the specialty before joining the plant (seniorityYears), together with the interview results, sets the initial value of skillHours.

Final hourly rate:

hRate = Rbase × Kedu × Ktenure

More on skillHours

The skillHours indicator reflects experience in the skills of the specialty and has the greatest effect on the hourly rate. Only hours confirmed by daily reports on completed tasks count towards it, so pay growth is tied to work actually done.

It is easiest to show how this works with examples.

Example 1: a student or graduate joins the enterprise

The intern starts with zero experience (skillHours = 0) and completes an internship of, say, 6 months. At the norm of 120 hours a month that is 720 hours, so after six months the intern reaches the Junior level. Throughout this time their hourly rate grows every month according to the rate grid.

Example 2: a Junior-level specialist joins the plant

The interview establishes the entry level of knowledge and experience against the list of skills of the specialty. Suppose the candidate is assigned an initial skillHours = 720. After six months (another 720 hours) they reach the Middle level, and until then their hourly rate grows monthly according to the Junior rate grid.

Example 3: a Middle-level specialist joins the plant

The interview establishes the entry level of knowledge and experience against the list of skills of the specialty. Suppose the candidate is assigned an initial skillHours = 1440. After a year (another 1440 hours) they reach the Senior level, and until then their hourly rate grows monthly according to the Middle rate grid.
