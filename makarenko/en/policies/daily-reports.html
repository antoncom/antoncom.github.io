Daily Reports Policy in force

Full text of the policy · Team Regulations

The vast majority of our employees work remotely.

The work is built on trust, because neither the team lead nor, still less, senior management has the wish or the time to stand behind anyone's back checking every step.

At the same time, there has not yet been a single showdown in which the director vented irritation at a subordinate with a reprimand like “what am I paying you for”.

This level of mutual understanding is achieved with an essentially simple approach: every employee regularly and briefly reports what is happening in their area of work. We call such a message a report.

The essence of reports

A report is a brief, precise, well-founded statement of what you did, how you did it and why, and how much time it took.

Those who served in the army may recall that for a muddled report — that is, an incoherent account to a superior — you could “get it in the teeth”, and not only figuratively.

In youth this seems excessive strictness. In maturity one comes to understand that a precise and clear report means saving the precious time of a superior on whom the well-being of the whole team largely depends.

Examples of good reports

  1. Bought two “modem” SIM cards from Beeline and MTS for testing the “Send SMS” service. Expenses: 1,200 roubles (receipt scan 1, receipt scan 2).
  2. Sent a router by CDEK to employee gan@ (photo of the kit), (shipping receipt). Shipping cost 758 roubles.
  3. Answered pns@'s questions (see the report): tsmsms_mutex.txt. I think we should hold a technical council with kms@.
  4. Finished writing an instruction for engineers and a presentation for management on AI. Document: “Using AI in Software Development” (detailed, for engineers). Presentation: “Using AI in Software Development” (brief presentation). I kept the pricing calculations for paid versions only in the document; they are not in the presentation. On the whole, the free versions can be used.

Examples of poor reports

No.ReportComments
1Bought Beeline, MTS SIMsUnclear what for. No receipts attached for reimbursement of expenses.

3Had problems comparing changes in configs. In both systems. Trying to figure it out..The message makes clear there is a problem — but what is it?
If you don't have time to write the report at the end of the day, better not to write it at all. Put it off until the next day and, having thought it over in the morning with a fresh mind, state briefly what the problem is.
4Analysing the chat with suppliers:
— we believe the K401 may turn out to have both an Android version via AOSP and, our option, an open-linux version via Yocto;
— it remains to understand what support they provide for this Yocto build;
— and to find out whether K401 support can be added to OpenWrt.
Technical research: forming a general picture of how Linux build systems for embedded systems work, how they are configured and how they differ		Several comments:
1. No completed action. If you did something, write so: “analysed the chat”. Try to use verbs of completed action (“did” instead of “doing”; “analysed” instead of “analysing”).
2. Inconsistent thinking. Make sure the account is consistent: first report that you analysed the supplier chat; then explain that the technical research revealed certain facts (about Android, OpenWrt support, etc.). Then say what you intend to do next (e.g. “next I'll find out what support the supplier provides for the Yocto build”).
5Tested the L2TP tunnel for connection stability over a long period — found no problems.No justification. Strive to make the statement in the report well-founded. Here a couple of screenshots confirming the test would be enough.
6Decided, as part of the L2TP tunnel work, to start implementing the “related” L2TPv3 tunnel. Started the corresponding technical research.No justification. If during technical research you reached a decision about how to continue the task, it must be justified. For example, it is enough to add “because…” (with a link to an article explaining why this should be considered the right approach).
7(a long account of two weeks of work with no intermediate result)Several comments:
1. Lack of discipline. Make it a rule to report every 45, 90, 180 or 360 minutes of mental work (whichever interval suits you), so as not to lose freshness of thought, not to forget important nuances, and to show intermediate results.
2. Many words — nothing to show. The team lead and senior managers have only 3–5 minutes to read a report and assess the result. Producing such a “scroll” and expecting someone to analyse your two weeks of activity without any sign of completion is naivety bordering on disrespect for other people's time.
3. It looks like an invoice for two weeks of work without even an intermediate result. Since we pay for all actual time, the work of the employee who wrote this report was, of course, paid. But after several such “messages”, naturally, the cooperation ended.
